Persistent Text RPG — Master Game Blueprint
Version 0.2 • Prototype handoff • September 2026
1. Vision
•
Persistent multiplayer, text-only RPG inspired by classic MUDs.
•
Setting: high fantasy with a science-fiction/futuristic twist—swords, magic and traditional fantasy life alongside mysterious advanced relics, machines and scientific technology.
•
Keyboard-first play; no graphics or minimap required. Exploration, mystery, danger, progression and rare loot are central.
2. Exploration & Commands
•
Movement: NORTH/N, SOUTH/S, EAST/E, WEST/W; UP/DOWN or special exits where appropriate.
•
LOOK/L describes the current room, visible objects, creatures and players. LOOK <target> examines something more closely. LOOK <direction> attempts to see into an adjacent area before entering.
•
Visibility depends on darkness, terrain, obstruction, weather/visibility rules and potentially character abilities or light sources.
•
Other core commands: INVENTORY/I, EQUIPMENT, ATTACK, BASH, FLEE, GET, DROP, USE, EQUIP, BUY, SELL, HELP. Multiplayer communication/group commands come later.
3. Character & Progression
•
Prototype starts with one race (Human) and one class (Warrior); both systems must be data-driven so more can be added later.
•
Characters gain XP from enemies; level thresholds increase appropriate stats such as maximum HP and can unlock skills.
•
Progress saves automatically: character, location, XP/level, gold, inventory, equipment and learned abilities. Architecture should permit multiple characters per account later.
4. Combat
•
Combat is fast and automatic rather than slow menu-turn combat. It alternates: player basic attack, about 2 seconds, enemy attack, about 2 seconds, repeating. Timing must be owner-adjustable.
•
Players can type active commands while automatic attacks continue. BASH is the prototype Warrior ability: stronger than a normal attack with a cooldown.
•
Hit/damage outcomes use hidden random rolls modified by stats, gear, skills and enemy attributes. The game engine—not AI—owns rolls, HP, timing, cooldowns and outcomes.
•
Enemies may be aggressive (attack on entry), passive/neutral, defensive or future behavior types. FLEE is a probabilistic, configurable escape attempt.
5. Inventory, Shops & Loot
•
Inventory has limited bag capacity that can later be upgraded. Weapons and armor modify combat effectiveness.
•
Include merchants such as a potion/general shop.
•
Enemies use weighted loot tables: gold, consumables, normal equipment and occasional uncommon/rare exciting drops. Tougher enemies generally offer better rewards without guaranteeing them.
•
Item stats, prices, drop chances, bag capacity and economy values must be editable.
6. Death
•
Death should matter without being excessively punishing. Prototype starting value: lose about 10% of carried gold; make this configurable.
•
Also roll for a configurable chance of losing one eligible non-equipped inventory item. Key/important items can later be protected.
•
Death should discourage using suicide merely as a convenient way to escape being lost. Respawn at the town/safe starting area for the prototype.
7. Persistent World & Multiplayer
•
Eventually multiple players can occupy the world simultaneously, see one another, communicate, group and cooperate in combat.
•
Enemy state is shared. A killed creature remains absent until its respawn time.
•
Use timestamps (for example defeated_at and respawn_at) instead of continuously simulating an empty world. This should make the world feel always-on while minimizing hosting/processing.
•
The prototype may be solo-first, but the architecture must support multiplayer later without a major rewrite.
8. Owner/Admin Editing — Essential
•
The game must be data-driven. Rooms, exits, descriptions, enemies, HP, damage, XP, respawn times, weapons, armor, items, prices, loot tables, races, classes, skills, cooldowns and progression values must be separate from core engine logic.
•
Changing a goblin from 25 HP to 35 HP must not require changing combat code.
•
Design toward a simple owner-only admin editor to add/edit areas, connect exits, create enemies/items, change difficulty and loot, edit shops and tune progression.
•
Validate edits and eventually provide backup/change history.
9. Version 0.1 Prototype World
•
The player begins in the CENTER SQUARE of a small futuristic-fantasy frontier town.
•
Build only enough world to prove the game: Center Square, a few nearby town locations, potion/general shop, road out of town, meadow/frontier area and a dark relic cave.
•
The relic cave mixes fantasy atmosphere with ancient scientific/technological remnants.
•
Include a few ordinary enemies, at least one aggressive goblin-style enemy, one non-aggressive creature and one mini-boss.
•
Include a small set of weapons, armor, potions and loot, including at least one uncommon/exciting possible drop.
•
The cave must demonstrate darkness, directional looking, detailed object inspection, combat, loot and respawning.
10. Prototype Success Criteria
•
Create a Human Warrior and enter Center Square; move and use LOOK, LOOK <target> and LOOK <direction>.
•
Buy/use a potion; manage inventory/equipment; fight aggressive and passive enemies.
•
Automatic alternating combat accepts BASH and FLEE and uses randomized server-controlled rolls.
•
Gain XP/gold/loot, level up, die with configured penalties, log out and return with progress preserved.
•
Enemy respawns use timestamps.
•
Owner can change enemy HP, weapon damage, room descriptions, loot chances and combat timing without modifying core combat code.
11. AI/Development Efficiency Rules
•
This blueprint is the source of truth. Do not redesign established requirements unless explicitly requested.
•
Do not build the whole world. Build and test the smallest vertical slice first and preserve working code between milestones.
•
Do not rewrite unrelated working components to fix a local issue.
•
Use deterministic code for dice, combat, saving, XP, inventory, cooldowns, loot and respawns. Runtime AI is not required for core gameplay.
•
AI may help draft descriptions, lore and structured content later. Define stable schemas before generating lots of content.
•
Optimize the early prototype for low/no-cost hosting and low processing requirements.
12. Open Decisions — Leave TBD
•
Game/world name and deeper lore; additional races/classes; full stat formulas; mana/stamina systems; final death item-loss probability; PvP; trading/banks/crafting/quests/guilds; dynamic weather/day-night; maximum group size; endgame; final production hosting stack.

14. Suggested Build Milestones
1.
Project skeleton, data schema, save/auth structure, command parser, Center Square, movement and LOOK.
2.
Connected prototype world, directional looking, darkness, inspectable objects, inventory, equipment and shop.
3.
Enemies, alternating combat, rolls, BASH, FLEE, potions, death, XP, leveling and loot.
4.
Persistent character/world state and timestamp enemy respawns.
5.
Owner-editable content/admin path; prove it by changing goblin HP and adding a room/item without changing engine code.
6.
Test/harden the slice, then evaluate multiplayer before expanding content.
