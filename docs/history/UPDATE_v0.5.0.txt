RELIC FRONTIER v0.5.0 — THE LIVING FRONTIER

START THE NEW VERSION
1. Stop any running game with Ctrl+C in its launcher.
2. Keep Relic_Frontier_v0.4.0 as your backup.
3. Open Start_Game.cmd inside Relic_Frontier_v0.5.0.
4. Check that the launcher and game both say v0.5.0. Refresh the browser
   with Ctrl+F5 if needed. The normal address is http://127.0.0.1:8765.

The supplied saved game is included unchanged. Your character's level, XP,
possessions, custom world, discoveries and timers carry forward. New save
fields are added at first launch. Always keep an old save with its old game;
do not copy a v0.5 save back into v0.4. No new starting lantern is added.

WHAT CHANGED

OWNER WORKSHOP
The editor opens to a dashboard. Category navigation, a record-name filter,
alphabetical selectors, collapsible forms and related-record links keep
editing manageable. Visual world map still opens rooms when you click their
blocks and still creates connected rooms and exports reference maps.

Use Add something to your world for an NPC, status effect or cooking recipe,
as well as the existing record types. New mechanics use normal forms:

- Rooms > Spawning, roaming, visibility & crafting
- Creatures > Status effects, resistance & harvesting
- Items & equipment > Hands, food, recall & effects
- Skills & spells > Combat initiation & status effects
- Characters > Races > Perception & resistance
- Status effects, NPCs & dialogue, Cooking recipes, Crafting & balance

Save world changes to apply your draft. Content saves retain a history copy.
Complete combat before saving world changes. Existing deadlines retain their
scheduled times; changed delays apply to subsequent events. Occupied extra
spawn slots and active effects cannot be removed until no longer in use.

COMBAT AND TARGETING
Basic attacks continue automatically. The existing 2-second alternating
player/enemy pulse is preserved: normally the player attacks every 4 seconds
and each engaged creature attacks every 4 seconds. This avoids doubling the
old combat speed. Combat & progression changes the pulse interval.

Several creatures can share a room. Each has its own health, effects,
identity and respawn slot. Hostile arrivals join an ongoing fight. Neutral
creatures join when attacked or manually targeted during combat. Combat
retargets engaged/hostile opponents when a target dies, leaves or is knocked
out; it does not automatically attack unrelated neutral wildlife.

  TARGET scarred copper beetle
  ATTACK watchful copper beetle
  CAST BASH
  FLEE

The creature panel offers Target and Attack buttons. Duplicate display names
gain persistent descriptive traits; ambiguous commands ask for a distinct
description. Manual selection remains until changed or invalidated.

Commands resolve immediately between automatic pulses, subject to cooldowns
and eligibility. They never reset the automatic attack schedule. There are no
turn-choice prompts. Combat still continues when the browser is closed.
Restarting the server schedules the next combat pulse in the future; it does
not replay an offline combat burst.

STATUS EFFECTS
Create reusable effects with damage, stun or slow mechanics. Burning and
poison can be separate damage effects with different names and messages.
Attach them to skills, weapons or creature attacks. Each attachment has its
own chance and optional duration override; 0 uses the effect's default.

Damage effects deal their configured damage on every combat pulse. Stun
prevents automatic attacks and skill use, but still permits target selection,
potions and flee attempts. Slow changes automatic attack recovery; rounding
to available actor pulses can extend the delay. A recovery already scheduled
by Slow may finish after the effect fades. Skills retain their own cooldowns.

Choose refresh, stack (up to a configurable cap), or ignore for repeat hits.
Stacking multiplies damage, not stun/slow strength. Application probability
is attachment chance x (1 - resistance / 100). Define creature resistance in
Creatures and player resistance in Races. Effects on weapons/skills apply
only after a successful damaging hit against a surviving opponent. No effect
is applied on a miss, dodge or kill. Effects pause outside combat and offline;
remaining combat pulses persist through save/restart. Death and capture clear
the affected creature/player's effects.

Messages accept {target}, {effect}, and {damage}. All are plain text.

DRAFT SKILLS
These are adjustable starting values; existing Bash and Knockout are intact.

Skill        Level  Eligibility  Cooldown  Initial behavior
Fireball       2    Mage          12 s     1.6x damage; 25% Scorched, 3 pulses
Lightning      4    Mage          16 s     2.0x damage; 15% Slowed, 4 pulses
Charge         2    Warrior       14 s     1.5x damage; may start a fight
Sharpening     2    Any           30 s     +2 weapon damage for 120 seconds
Enchanting     3    Any           15 s     Train proficiency; risky weapon work

All allow any race initially. Fireball and Lightning can also initiate combat.
Spells use the existing damage formula and cooldown resource model: class
roll + primary weapon + level bonus, multiplied by skill strength. There is
no mana or elemental damage-resistance model in this release; named status
resistances provide secondary-effect resistance. Charge does not move rooms.

  FIREBALL / LIGHTNING / CHARGE
  SHARPEN (or CAST SHARPENING)
  ENCHANT (or CAST ENCHANTING)

Sharpening and Enchanting require a right-hand weapon and no active combat.
They make that particular weapon an individual object. Other copies of the
same weapon template are unchanged. Individual weapons retain their changes
when dropped, stored in a chest, equipped or reloaded. Sharpening refreshes
its duration rather than stacking, and its timer includes offline time.

ENCHANTING DEFAULTS — ALL IN CRAFTING & BALANCE
- Each valid attempt costs 20 gold and grants 2 proficiency, up to 100.
- Success = 50% + proficiency x 0.4% - existing rank x 12%, clamped to 0–100%.
- Success adds 1 weapon damage and 1 rank; maximum rank is 3.
- A failed attempt has a 10% chance to destroy the weapon. Otherwise it loses
  1 damage, down to zero base weapon damage. Training still occurs.
- Invalid attempts spend no money, proficiency or cooldown.

HAND EQUIPMENT
  EQUIP iron sword RIGHT
  EQUIP iron sword LEFT
  EQUIP wooden buckler
  UNEQUIP LEFT / UNEQUIP RIGHT

The old WEAPON slot is retained internally as the right hand for compatibility.
The player interface labels the hand slots. Body armor remains separate.
A shield occupies the left hand and adds its armor value to incoming damage
reduction. A two-handed weapon occupies the right hand and requires the left
hand to be empty. Set Hands required in the item's additional fields.

Dual wielding initially requires Warrior level 3. The offhand contributes
35% of its weapon damage to the same attack, and dual wielding subtracts 0.10
from hit chance. It does not add another full class/level attack. Eligibility,
unlock level, contribution and penalty are editable in Crafting & balance.
A lone one-handed weapon can be used in either hand without the dual penalty.

SPAWNING AND ROAMING
Existing fixed enemies, weighted respawn pools and rare entry encounters keep
their old definitions and timers. Additional independent spawn groups work
alongside them. A group's permanent ID and slot count identify its saved
creatures. Use a fixed creature or weighted pool, 0–100% chance per attempt,
entry/timer trigger, respawn toggle, retry delay and roaming options.

Failed rolls produce nothing and still start the retry delay. A disabled
respawn permits the first successful appearance, then no replacement after
death/capture. Entry groups roll only on actual entry, never LOOK or polling.
Timer groups first initialize when the location is visited/evaluated; roaming
timer groups may initialize as the world clock evaluates them.

Roaming moves only across an existing exit to a checked, non-safe destination.
Include the home room among allowed destinations if the creature may return.
An empty destination list prevents movement. Fighting and unconscious
creatures do not roam. The home slot remains occupied while its creature is
away; death/capture schedules replacement at that original home.

Legacy enemies have separate respawn and roaming controls in the same room
panel. Their spawn chance applies to replacement attempts, while their fixed
starting enemy is preserved. Group rules support chance on initial appearance.
Rare legacy encounter rooms retain their encounter chance and cooldown.

Two light wildlife additions illustrate the system without increasing the
existing hostile populations: deer near Sundown Flats and turkeys near
Coppergrass Reach have a 35% entry chance, one slot each, 180-second retries,
and restricted 45-second roaming. All their values are editable.

NPCS
Tamsin is in Center Square as an example. LOOK TAMSIN or SAY HELLO TO TAMSIN.
NPC forms edit placement, appearance, description, dialogue and enabled
Look/Talk interactions. Dialogue matches complete phrases ignoring case and
extra spaces. Shops, quest chains and branching conversations remain future
work; the placed-NPC model is ready to be extended.

PERCEPTION
  LOOK NORTH 2
The default maximum distance is 3 rooms. Near, clear views are reliable.
Farther views roll: 85% + 2% per level after level 1 + racial bonus - 20% per
extra room - the strongest fog penalty along the observed path, clamped to
0–100%. Directional looking follows connected exits in the same direction.
Darkness, blocked outside views, sight limits, missing exits and failed checks
stop further details. Nearer visible rooms may be revealed before a distant
view fails. Looking does not create enemies or roll encounters.

Use room Fog / weather visibility penalty and Maximum sight through this room
to author weather/terrain restrictions. A sight limit of 0 prevents onward
view; obscured-from-outside prevents seeing into that room. Weather is
owner-authored, not a dynamically simulated weather cycle.

RECALL, FOOD AND COOKING
Anthony's General Store sells a 150-gold recall scroll, a 30-gold wooden
buckler and 3-gold prairie apples. RECALL or USE RECALL SCROLL returns to the
starting square and consumes one scroll. Combat or Recall disabled on the
current room blocks it. Blocked attempts preserve the scroll by default;
Crafting & balance can change that rule explicitly.

EAT <food> heals up to maximum HP. Food is preserved at full HP and when an
attempt is blocked. Defaults: outside combat, 8 seconds between meals.
Ordinary healing potions retain their previous behavior.

  HARVEST <creature description>
  COOK roast venison
  COOK roast turkey
  COOK toast beetle

Defeated deer, turkeys and copper beetles leave harvestable remains for 300
seconds. Each corpse is harvested once; a full bag preserves it. Capture and
killing released captives grant no harvesting rewards. Creature harvest
fields choose distinct output items and quantities. The Quiet Coil has a
cooking fire. Recipes choose input/output quantities, fire/tool/skill
requirements, success chance and whether failure consumes ingredients.
Initial cooking is guaranteed, needs a fire, and requires no tool or skill.
Venison heals 16 HP, turkey 12 HP, beetle 8 HP and apples 5 HP.

PRESERVATION AND LIMITS
The original 29 rooms, names, descriptions, IDs, map positions, starting
inventory, old creature stats, race/class choices and skill settings remain.
Changes to existing records are additive: shop stock, a cooking fire, beetle
harvest yields and the two wildlife spawn rules. Your original save bytes
are included in the distribution and migrate on first launch.

This remains one local character with a loopback-only browser server. New
player effects/proficiency/target state live on the player, and creatures,
home slots and corpses live in world state with independent identities.
This prepares useful boundaries without implementing multiplayer, accounts,
roles, full NPC shops/quests, technology regions or Moon content.

Balance is a conservative starting point, not a claim of exhaustive playtest
balance. All new gameplay balance settings described above are editor-managed.
Use a separate copy for experiments. A later player-created save must always
stay with its matching content and game version.
